# Background: a recorded moment surrogate

The background replays the saved training history of a small **3 → 5 → 5 → 2** neural network. The network is trained offline by `node scripts/build-monte-carlo-training.mjs`. The browser only interpolates saved weights and runs a small forward pass; it does not train a model, fetch data, or use an ML library.

The inputs are a drift parameter, volatility, and time horizon. The outputs are the terminal log-return mean and log standard deviation, standardized for training. Hidden layers use `tanh`; the output layer is linear. Playback converts the predicted mean and positive standard deviation back to the one-year drift/volatility parameters used to draw the blue paths. The blue paths and grey reference use consistent underlying randomness so parameter changes remain visible.

The training targets come from **4,096 seeded Monte Carlo terminal log returns for each of 100 scenarios** on a drift/volatility/horizon grid. Samples follow geometric Brownian motion in log space, `(mu − sigma² / 2) × time + sigma × sqrt(time) × normalNoise`. Full-batch Adam runs for 2,400 iterations, retaining 12 checkpoints with their actual weights, biases and whole-grid training MSE. Re-running the script reproduces the checked-in JSON artifact without external dependencies.

For the displayed one-year reference (`mu = 0.06`, `sigma = 0.36`), the model starts at approximately `mu = −0.2696`, `sigma = 0.1530`, and finishes at `mu = 0.06153`, `sigma = 0.36587`. Whole-grid standardized training MSE falls from **0.693704** to **0.000327**. The finite simulated target for that specific scenario has mean `−0.0072842` and standard deviation `0.3611567`; finite-sample targets and the learned approximation need not exactly equal the analytical reference. The actual fitting history includes a small overshoot rather than a fabricated monotonically improving curve.

These figures describe the training grid, which includes the displayed scenario. They are **not a held-out benchmark, evidence of financial forecasting skill, or a claim that a neural network is needed to calculate these known moments**. This is a reproducible illustration of learning a simulator's summary statistics. The arrows and pacing present the recorded training process schematically; no live gradient computation happens on the page.

`modelState(progress).loss` measures the currently displayed model's squared error against that scenario's simulated mean/log-standard-deviation targets, averaged over its two normalized outputs. Checkpoint `.loss` values separately record whole-grid training MSE. Between checkpoints, both the model's output and displayed loss are computed from interpolated real weights, rather than interpolating an invented prediction or loss curve.
